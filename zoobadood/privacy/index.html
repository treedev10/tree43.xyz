Zoobadood VR Privacy Policy

Effective September 29, 2026 · Last updated October 3, 2026

This policy explains what information Zoobadood VR (“the game”) handles, why, who else is involved, how long anything is kept, and what you can do about it. It also explains how to report another player. The game is made by Tree43 Interactive (“we”, “us”).

1. The short version

  • You don't create an account with us, and we never ask for your email address. In online games, other players see your Meta display name (or Meta username) above your head, which the game reads from your Meta account through Meta's platform service. Nothing else from your Meta account is used.
  • Offline modes (practice, the test area and the bot match) run entirely on your headset and send nothing anywhere.
  • Online games run through Unity Gaming Services. To connect you, Unity gives you a random player ID and sees your IP address. Your head and hand movements are sent to the other players in your game while you play, and aren't stored.
  • Online games have text chat. Messages go to the players in your game only, pass through the host's headset, and aren't stored or recorded. You can mute or report anyone.
  • Voice chat isn't in the game yet. When it is added, it will be off until you allow microphone access, other players can mute you, and you can mute and report them. We won't record voice chat.
  • We don't sell your information, don't show ads and don't use analytics or tracking.
  • You report players from inside the game. Your Meta Quest's own reporting tools work too. See Reporting a player.

2. What we collect

Stored only on your headset

Your game settings, such as turn speed and whether the performance display is on. These never leave your headset and are deleted when you uninstall the game.

When you play online

Online play (Play, Private Room and Join Room) uses Unity Gaming Services, run by Unity Technologies. The first time you go online, the game signs in to Unity anonymously. This involves:

  • Anonymous player ID. A random ID that Unity creates for your copy of the game, plus a sign-in token stored on your headset so you keep the same ID next time. It isn't linked to your name, your email or your Meta account.
  • IP address. Unity's servers see your IP address when you connect, as any online service does. Other players don't: game traffic goes through Unity's relay servers rather than directly between headsets.
  • Game room details. While a game is open, Unity holds its room code, whether it's public or private, the number of players, the game version and the player IDs of the people in it. If players vote someone out of a room, the host's game remembers that player's ID until the room closes, so they can't rejoin it.
  • Gameplay data. While you're in a game, your head and hand positions and your in-game actions (catching, throwing, losing an arm) are sent to the other players so they can see you. This data passes through Unity's relay servers in real time and isn't recorded by us.
  • Your Meta name. On a Meta Quest, the game asks Meta's platform service for your Meta display name (or, if that isn't available, your Meta username) and shows it to the other players in your game, on your name tag and in the player list. It also confirms with Meta that your account is entitled to the game. The game doesn't receive your email, friends list, profile picture or any other Meta account details, and we don't store your name.
  • Text chat. Messages you send with the CHAT panel go to the other players in your game through the host's headset, in real time. The host's game hides some offensive words and limits how fast messages can be sent. Messages are kept in each player's game only while the room is open, and aren't stored or recorded by us or by Unity.
  • Basic technical information. Unity may receive standard connection details such as your device type, operating system and game version, which it needs to run the service.

When you report a player

An in-game report sends the reported player's anonymous player ID, the reason you picked and the time to Unity's moderation service, where we review it. Unity knows which anonymous player ID sent it because you're signed in. We use reports only to look into them.

When you contact us

If you email us (for a privacy request or an appeal), we receive your email address and whatever you choose to send. We use it only to deal with your message.

3. What we don't collect

  • Your name, email address (unless you email us), phone number or postal address.
  • Your Meta email address, friends list, profile picture or other profile details. The only thing the game reads from your Meta account is your display name (or username), as described above.
  • Precise location, contacts, photos or files.
  • Eye tracking, face tracking, hand-tracking camera data or images of your room. The game uses your controllers' positions only.
  • Analytics, advertising IDs or any tracking across other apps and websites.

4. Voice chat Coming later

Voice chat is not in the current version of the game. We plan to add it, and this section describes how it will work so there are no surprises. If our plans change, we'll update this policy before the update that adds voice chat is released.

  • Microphone permission. The game won't use your microphone until you allow it in your headset's permission prompt. You can turn the permission off at any time in your Quest settings, and the rest of the game will still work.
  • Who hears you. Only the players in the same game room as you, while you're both in it.
  • How it's delivered. We expect to use Unity's voice service (Vivox), which carries your voice to the other players in real time. Unity processes the audio to deliver it and doesn't keep recordings for us.
  • No recording. We won't record, store or listen to voice chat. If that ever changes (for example to review reports), we'll say so here first and tell you in the game before it applies to you.
  • Controls. You'll be able to mute yourself, mute any other player, and report a player from inside the game.

Please don't share personal information (your full name, address, school, phone number or social media accounts) in text chat or voice chat. Other players are strangers unless you invited them.

5. How the information is used

We and Unity use the information above only to:

  • connect you to online games and keep them running;
  • let you create private rooms and join them with a code;
  • show your movements, your Meta name and your chat messages (and, once added, carry your voice) to the other players in your game;
  • investigate reports and keep online play safe, including restricting access for players who break the rules;
  • answer your messages;
  • meet legal obligations and protect people from harm.

We don't use your information for advertising or profiling, and we don't make automated decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects.

6. Who it's shared with

  • Unity Technologies runs our online services (Authentication, Lobby, Relay, Moderation for player reports and, later, Vivox voice) on our behalf. Unity handles this data under its own privacy policy.
  • Other players in your game see your Meta name, your avatar's movements and actions and the chat messages you send, and, once voice chat is added, hear you if your microphone is on.
  • Meta provides your display name and checks your entitlement to the game through its platform service, and distributes the game and handles purchases through the Meta Horizon Store under Meta's privacy policy. We don't receive your payment details. If you use Meta's reporting tools, Meta handles that report.
  • Authorities, when the law requires it, or when we believe in good faith that sharing is needed to protect someone from serious harm (see Reporting a player).
  • A new owner, if Tree43 Interactive or the game is sold or merged, in which case this policy would continue to protect your information.

We don't sell your personal information, and we don't share it for cross-context behavioral advertising.

8. Reporting a player

Harassment, hate speech, threats, sexual content, cheating and anything that puts someone in danger aren't allowed in Zoobadood VR. If someone behaves like this, you can:

Leave and block them out

  • Press EXIT to leave the game at any time.
  • Start a vote kick: open the player list (PLAYERS on your panel, or B on your controller), pick the player and choose VOTE KICK. Everyone else in the room gets a vote. If most of them agree, the player is removed and can't rejoin that room. The host can't be voted out; if the host is the problem, leave and join another room.
  • Mute the player from the same player list: their chat messages stop showing for you straight away (and, once voice chat is added, you stop hearing them). Muting only affects you.
  • Play in a private room so only people you've given the code to can join.

Report them

Reporting happens in the game. Open the player list (PLAYERS on your panel, or B on your controller), pick the player and choose REPORT, then pick a reason. The report comes straight to us with the details listed in What we collect, so you don't need to note down codes or times. The player isn't told who reported them.

You can also report a player, or the app itself, from your Quest's universal menu. Those reports go to Meta and follow Meta's own process.

What happens next

We review every report, usually within 7 days and sooner for anything involving a child or a threat of harm. Depending on what we find, we may warn the player, remove their access to voice chat, or block their player ID from online play for a period or permanently. Reports are anonymous, so we can't reply to them individually, and we don't share what happened to the other player.

If action was taken against you and you think it was a mistake, email us with the subject “Appeal” and we'll look at it again.

If someone is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first.

If you see anything that sexually exploits a child, report it in the game and to the NCMEC CyberTipline (US) or your local police. We report child sexual abuse material to NCMEC and cooperate with law enforcement.

9. Your rights and choices

Depending on where you live, including the European Economic Area, the UK and US states such as California, you may have the right to:

  • know what personal information we hold about you and get a copy of it;
  • have it corrected or deleted;
  • object to or restrict how we use it;
  • take it elsewhere (data portability);
  • withdraw consent where we rely on it, such as microphone access;
  • not be treated differently for using any of these rights.

To use any of these rights, email tree43@zohomail.com with the subject “Privacy request”. We'll respond within 30 days. We may need to ask for information to confirm the request is yours. You can also have an authorised agent contact us for you.

Deleting your online data

Uninstalling the game deletes your settings, your sign-in token and your anonymous player ID from your headset. After that, nothing Unity still holds can be linked back to you, your headset or your Meta account. If you'd also like Unity's copy of the anonymous ID deleted, email us. Because the ID isn't tied to your name or account, we may ask you to help us identify it (for example the approximate date and time you last played online).

Complaints

If you're unhappy with how we've handled your information, please tell us first so we can try to fix it. If you're in the EEA or UK, you can also complain to your local data protection authority (in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office).

10. Children

Zoobadood VR is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. Meta sets its own age requirements for Quest accounts, and parents of younger users can manage app access and permissions (including the microphone) through Meta's parental supervision tools.

If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information, for example by emailing us, contact us and we'll delete it.

11. Legal bases (EEA and UK)

If you're in the EEA or UK, we rely on these legal bases:

  • Performing our agreement with you: running the online games you choose to join, including relaying your movements (and, later, your voice) to other players.
  • Legitimate interests: keeping online play safe and working, investigating reports, preventing abuse and cheating. We balance these against your rights, and you can object.
  • Consent: using your microphone for voice chat. You can withdraw it at any time by turning off the microphone permission.
  • Legal obligation: responding to lawful requests and reporting illegal content where required.

12. International transfers

Unity's servers are located in several countries, including the United States, and the relay server used for a game is picked based on the players' locations. When information is transferred out of the EEA or UK, Unity relies on recognised safeguards such as the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses.

13. Security

Online traffic between the game and Unity's services is encrypted. We keep the information we hold to a minimum, and only Tree43 Interactive staff who handle reports and privacy requests can access it. No system is perfectly secure, but if a breach ever affected your information, we'd tell you and the relevant authorities as the law requires.

14. Changes to this policy

We'll update this policy when the game changes how it handles information, including before voice chat is released. The date at the top shows when it last changed. For significant changes, we'll also tell you in the game or in the store listing's update notes.
